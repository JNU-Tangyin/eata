\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section evaluates EATA and addresses the research questions of Section~\ref{sec:intro}. We evaluate on representative constituent stocks (N=345) from the S\&P 500 index, covering Technology, Finance, and Healthcare. The evaluation period spans February 8, 2013 to February 7, 2018, encompassing post-crisis recovery, QE tapering, the oil-price collapse, Brexit, a strong bull market, and the early-2018 correction. Daily OHLCV data is split temporally into a training period (Feb 8, 2013 to Jun 30, 2016) and an out-of-sample testing period (Jul 1, 2016 to Feb 7, 2018).

\subsection{Baselines}
We compare EATA against 11 baselines across five categories:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Traditional}: Buy \& Hold, MACD (tuned).
    \item \textbf{Statistical}: ARIMA (5,1,0).
    \item \textbf{Machine Learning}: LightGBM, GBDT, Genetic Programming (via Operon)~\cite{burlacu2020operon}.
    \item \textbf{Deep Learning}: LSTM, Transformer.
    \item \textbf{Reinforcement Learning}: PPO, A2C, TD3, DDPG (FinRL).
\end{itemize}
Hyperparameters follow standard settings from the cited implementations and the literature.

\subsection{Main Results: Profitability vs.\ Black-Box (RQ3)}

\subsubsection{Performance Comparison}
\textbf{Hypothesis (RQ3).} Profit-aware symbolic strategies can match or exceed black-box baselines in risk-adjusted returns while remaining intrinsically interpretable.

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports performance averaged across our universe. EATA achieves a \textbf{Sharpe ratio of 0.76} with an annualized return of 21.2\%, competitive with black-box baselines while maintaining full interpretability.

\subsection{Mechanism Analysis: Wasserstein vs.\ MSE (RQ1)}

\subsubsection{Return Distribution and Risk-Return Trade-off}
\textbf{Hypothesis (RQ1).} Optimizing distributional divergence (Wasserstein) rather than point-wise error (MSE) can improve tail-risk control and yield a more favorable return asymmetry.

Figure~\ref{fig:dist} shows EATA's return distribution with positive skewness (skewness = 1.14) and a mean daily return of 0.076\% (annualized 21.2\%); a risk-return scatter (omitted for brevity) confirms that EATA clusters in the high-return/moderate-risk region. Distribution-aware objectives penalize mismatches across the entire return distribution, whereas MSE is dominated by small central errors; since the left tail dominates realized utility under drawdown constraints, the distribution-aware objective reduces catastrophic losses.

\textbf{Finding (RQ1: Component Importance).} The ablation reveals a clear hierarchy. \textbf{MCTS is the most critical component}: removing it collapses the Sharpe ratio from 0.76 to 0.19 ($-75\%$), indicating that lookahead search is indispensable and neural policy alone cannot handle complex market dynamics. \textbf{Exploration is also crucial} (Sharpe 0.43, $-43\%$). \textbf{Wasserstein distance is valuable}: replacing it with simple MAE reduces the Sharpe ratio to 0.22 ($-71\%$), confirming that distributional alignment beats point-wise error minimization. The memory module (0.15) and neural network (0.45) contribute moderately, with the neural variant's relative contribution varying across market regimes.

\subsection{Interpretability and Search Efficiency (RQ2)}

\subsubsection{Search Efficiency and Pareto Frontier}
Neural guidance accelerates symbolic search: the guided version reaches a high Sharpe ratio much faster than pure MCTS (Figure~\ref{fig:eff_pareto}a). A Pareto-frontier analysis (Figure~\ref{fig:eff_pareto}b) shows that EATA achieves superior performance with moderate complexity: 6 valid symbolic expressions with an average Sharpe ratio of 0.824 and 9--17 AST nodes, versus GP's average 0.487 with most expressions degenerating to trivial constants. This 1.69$\times$ advantage at moderate complexity is valuable in regulated environments where overly complex rules are hard to audit.

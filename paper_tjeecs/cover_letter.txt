We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "EATA: Distribution-aware symbolic discovery for explainable trading" for consideration for publication in the Turkish Journal of Electrical Engineering and Computer Sciences.

Contribution

Algorithmic trading faces a fundamental tension between predictive performance and interpretability: deep neural predictors achieve strong accuracy but remain opaque, while symbolic regression produces readable formulas yet optimizes proxy objectives misaligned with economic utility. In this work, we present EATA, a neural-guided symbolic regression framework that aligns the objective, hypothesis space, and training signal with trading profitability:

1. Profit-aware objective. We formulate rule discovery as distribution matching via the 1-Wasserstein distance, which assigns uniform weight to all quantiles (including tails), provides adversarial robustness through its Kantorovich–Rubinstein dual, and provably bounds CVaR discrepancies (Proposition 2).
2. Neural-guided grammar search. A three-headed Policy-Value-Profit network (PVPNet) coupled with Monte Carlo Tree Search explores a finance-grounded, decoupled grammar with bounded module libraries, keeping the search auditable under regime shifts.
3. Comprehensive validation. On 345 S&P 500 constituents (2013–2018), EATA achieves an annualized return of 21.2%, a Sharpe ratio of 0.76, and a maximum drawdown of 17%, outperforming strong baselines (LSTM, Transformer, LightGBM, FinRL, GP) while retaining full interpretability; ablations confirm the necessity of each component.

Fit with the Journal

This work lies at the intersection of computer science, information technology, and intelligent decision systems — precisely the scope of TJEECS. The framework addresses a real-world intelligent-system design problem (automatic, interpretable decision rule discovery from financial time series) and demonstrates how machine learning and control-oriented search can be combined into a deployable, auditable decision agent.
